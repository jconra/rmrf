№25 — THE NIGHT EVERYTHING GOT A GATE

2026-08-08 · momentum that did not break the alarms, a null result worth keeping, and two bugs that were hiding inside a measurement

Three changes went to a 240-seed A/B tonight and two of them shipped. The interesting part is not the verdicts. It is that in every case the number that mattered was not the one the change was about.

Vehicles had no acceleration at all

The ask was a Valkyrie that winds up slowly and then out-runs everything. Looking for the acceleration term to change turned up the real state of things:

// Locomotion.js — the throttle is one of exactly three values
const fwd = a > PIVOT ? 0 : a > EASE ? 0.5 : 1;

// Vehicles.js — and the throttle IS the speed
const d = forward * eff * dt;

No velocity state anywhere. Every machine in the game reached full pelt in one frame and stopped just as instantly. So the request was not a stat tweak, it was introducing momentum to the chassis — and once it exists, every vehicle gets a character:

chassis0 → 90% topwhy
lurcherinstantsix legs push off the ground. No drivetrain to spin up, and going exactly where you point it with no turning circle is the whole identity of the chassis
firebrat0.7snimble and light
valkyrie2.4swinds up, then out-runs everything — top speed 16 → 22
jotun2.5sa rolling fortress should visibly heave itself into motion

Braking is 2.5× acceleration, because every arrival radius in the game — siege stands, camp spots, the elevator pad, locomote's settle distance — was written when a vehicle could stop dead in a single frame.

The risk was named before the run, which is what made the result readable

The Driver has two stuck detectors, and both compare ground covered against commanded throttle × nominal speed. A momentum chassis cannot deliver that while it is still spooling up. A Jotun setting off from rest might therefore read as wedged — and the stuck alarm ends in self-destruct. So the thing to watch was never the win rate.

Scuttles did rise, 17 → 27. Then the breakdown by chassis:

chassiscontrol+momentum+repair
lurcher122322
firebrat212
jotun302

The entire increase is Lurchers — the one chassis with accel: null, whose locomotion is bit-identical to control. And the Jotun, the specific worry, went to zero. The repair build, which touches locomotion not at all, shows the same Lurcher rise, and that is the line that closes it: this is a pre-existing Lurcher problem whose count swings with any change to match timing.

Which leaves a finding nobody went looking for. The Lurcher is the chassis that gets stuck — 12/17, 23/27 and 22/26 of all scuttles across the three builds. It is the omni chassis. It has no turning circle. It should be the least likely thing on the island to wedge itself.

The cost, stated plainly: matches run 24s longer with momentum, 358s → 382s, +6.7%. That is not noise — it is the mean over 230+ matches, and it is the honest consequence of units spending real time reaching speed instead of arriving at it.

A null result is a result

The repair mission needed weights: how hard a damaged unit should want to heal, and how much that should scale with being near help. A 1200-match head-to-head over a 4× range of both knobs came back like this:

confighpUrgenearMaxwin rate
huge10451.6%
eagerN102.451.1%
hard6450.4%
soft62.449.4%
eager10148.9%
flat6148.6%

Three points of spread at ~400 games each. That is noise, and the temptation is to crown "huge" anyway and call the weights tuned. They are not tuned. Win rate cannot see this knob, so the honest move is to say so and then choose on behaviour instead: hpUrge 10 / nearMax 4 is the strongest near/far contrast, which is the actual design goal — heal to full while standing at the fob, don't cross the map for it until genuinely low.

The mission itself gated clean: 231 vs 232 resolved, and better on every secondary metric — nav alarms 81 → 69, swap loops 214 → 179, losses-with-nothing-to-show 42 → 37.

Two bugs that were hiding inside a measurement

The death-transition effect was built in the fire lab so it could be judged before it shipped. Bounding boxes said the flames covered 95% of the viewport. Reading the actual framebuffer said they lit 34% of the pixels. The profile is a hollow shell — transparent through its middle, tapering at top and bottom — because it was authored to look like a flame standing against a background, not like an opaque curtain. Seven flames across two ranks bought 38%. So the flames do motion and shape, and a gradient glow field behind them carries the coverage: 0.3% → 8% → 45% → 100%.

And the sequencer's bass rendered at 116 peak against 0.13 for every other instrument. Every piece of evidence pointed at the Web Audio filter: static cutoff fine, same value via setValueAtTime catastrophic, resonance irrelevant, direction-dependent. The filter was innocent. The fault was one line, three files away:

freqPeak: (n.freqPeak ?? 0) * r

That reads like a harmless default. It is not. The filter chooses its behaviour by presence — freqPeak != null is true for zero — so turning an absent freqPeak into a present zero pushed every plain static-cutoff filter into the three-point sweep branch, which then ramped the cutoff to 0 Hz. A lowpass collapsing to zero is degenerate, and when the ramp carried it back up, everything it had stored came out at once. The peak landed at ~0.15s, which is the 0.08s default attack plus settling. It was in the timing the whole time.

Both of those spent an hour looking like a bug in somebody else's code. Both were ours, and both were found by printing what the thing actually returns rather than reasoning about what it ought to.
